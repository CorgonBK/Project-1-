Challenges, Limitations & Privacy

Despite impressive laboratory results, vision-based fall detection still faces significant practical obstacles.

1. Privacy

A camera in a bedroom or bathroom is understandably sensitive. Successful systems therefore:

  • Process video locally whenever possible
  • Transmit only skeletons, silhouettes, or heavily redacted clips
  • Require explicit consent and give residents control over the system

The University of Alberta / Spxtrm work explicitly highlighted that seniors care deeply about not having their physical appearance shown to operators.[2]

2. False Alarms

Sitting down quickly, lying on a bed, or bending to pick something up can look similar to a fall. High false-positive rates cause “alert fatigue” and lead users to disable the system. Common mitigations include:

  • Requiring a short period of low motion after the potential fall
  • Using multi-frame temporal models instead of single-frame rules
  • Combining vision with other sensors (accelerometer, pressure mat, radar)

3. Environmental Robustness

  • Occlusion – furniture, blankets, or another person can hide the faller
  • Lighting – night-time, strong backlight, or shadows degrade pose estimation
  • Camera placement – ceiling or high-wall views work best; low angles struggle
  • Multiple people – the system must decide which person is the resident of interest

4. Computational & Power Constraints

Running deep models continuously on a small edge device generates heat and consumes power. Designers therefore use:

  • Model quantization (INT8)
  • Event-triggered activation (only analyze when motion is present)
  • Very lightweight architectures (MobileNet-style backbones, tiny LSTMs)

5. Evaluation Gaps

Many published accuracies are measured on acted falls by young adults in laboratory settings. Real falls by frail older adults look different, and public datasets still under-represent this population. Consequently, reported numbers should be treated with caution until large-scale real-world trials are available.

Limitation summary: Vision-based systems are powerful but not yet perfect. Privacy, false alarms, and real-world robustness remain active research problems.
