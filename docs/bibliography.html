Annotated Bibliography

References are listed in the order they first appear in the tutorial. Each entry includes a short synopsis and a reliability note.

[1] Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) – Older Adult Falls Data
CDC, National Center for Injury Prevention and Control. Updated periodically.
Synopsis: Authoritative public-health statistics on the incidence, cost, and consequences of falls among adults aged 65 and older in the United States.
Reliability: High. Official U.S. government public-health agency. Data are widely cited in both clinical and engineering literature.
[2] “Using AI to protect the elderly”
Katie Willis, University of Alberta Faculty of Science News, 24 October 2018.
Synopsis: Describes the collaboration between the University of Alberta Multimedia Research Centre (Irene Cheng) and Spxtrm AI. The system uses deep-learning computer vision and motion classification to detect falls in real time, send caregiver alerts, and provide privacy-protected (redacted) video clips.
Reliability: High for an overview article. Published by the university itself; the technical claims are consistent with later academic and industry descriptions of the same project. Primary research papers from the group would provide deeper algorithmic detail.
[3] MediaPipe Pose / BlazePose documentation and papers
Google MediaPipe team. Official documentation and associated research publications (2019–present).
Synopsis: Describes a lightweight, real-time pose estimation solution that runs on mobile and edge devices and outputs 33 body landmarks. Widely used as a building block for fall-detection prototypes.
Reliability: High. Produced by a major industrial research group; the models are open and have been independently reproduced by many researchers.
[4] Lightweight YOLO-based fall detection models (YOLOv8 / YOLOv10 variants)
Various authors, 2024–2026 (examples include Mobile-Centric YOLOv8 systems and Fast-YOLO-FD).
Synopsis: Series of papers that adapt modern single-stage detectors for person and pose estimation, then classify falls. Emphasis is on small model size, high frame rate, and deployment on edge hardware.
Reliability: Moderate to high. Peer-reviewed or preprint literature; performance numbers should be checked against the exact dataset and evaluation protocol used.
[5] Skeleton-based LSTM fall detection on edge devices
Recent academic works (2024–2026) on optimized LSTM pipelines for ambient assisted living.
Synopsis: Demonstrates that a pose estimator followed by a carefully optimized LSTM can achieve strong accuracy and low false-alarm rates while running locally on inexpensive hardware, preserving privacy and surviving network outages.
Reliability: Moderate to high when published in peer-reviewed venues. Laboratory results are promising; large-scale home trials are still relatively scarce.
[6] Commercial ambient monitoring in senior living (illustrative)
Industry reports and news features on silhouette / gait-monitoring systems installed in senior residences (e.g., 2024–2025 coverage).
Synopsis: Describes real-world deployments that monitor gait and posture, detect falls, and present only abstracted visual information to staff.
Reliability: Moderate. Useful for understanding current practice, but marketing claims should be distinguished from independent evaluation.
